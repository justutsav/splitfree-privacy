The short version

  • SplitFree shows no ads and contains no analytics or advertising SDKs. It never reads your advertising ID.
  • Your data is not sold, and it is not shared with advertisers or data brokers.
  • People see an expense only if they are part of it.
  • You sign in with a one-time code sent to your email, or with your Google account. SplitFree stores no password.
  • You can delete your account at any time from inside the app.

What stays on your phone

Camera. Used only to scan QR codes: a friend's or a group's SplitFree code, or a shop's UPI QR, including from the Scan & pay widget on your home screen. The camera picture is read on your phone and is never uploaded or stored. If you do not allow the camera, you can pick a QR image from your gallery instead.

Photos you choose. Only the picture you pick is uploaded, made smaller first, as your profile picture or a payment screenshot. SplitFree does not look through your gallery.

A copy of your data for offline use. The app keeps the data it last loaded on your phone, so it opens quickly and still shows your balances without a connection. Signing out wipes it, and it is left out of Android's cloud backups.

Invite links. If you installed SplitFree from a friend's or a group's invite link, the app reads that invite from Google Play once, on first launch, so it can open the request for you.

Never touched. Location, contacts, microphone, SMS, call logs, calendar, health data and your advertising ID. The app does not ask for permission to any of them.

Payments and UPI

SplitFree does not move money. It never sees your bank account, card, balance or UPI PIN. When you settle up, SplitFree opens the UPI app you choose, such as Google Pay, PhonePe or Paytm, and keeps the other person's UPI ID ready to copy. You make the payment in that app, under its own privacy policy. When you pay a shop, the shop's QR code is passed to your UPI app exactly as scanned.

SplitFree does not learn whether a payment went through. It records only what you confirm: the amount, how you paid and, if you attach one, a screenshot.

Who can see what

People you share a friendship or a group with see your name, username, profile picture and UPI ID. In a friendship, both of you see every expense and settlement between you. In a group, members see the group's balances, but each person sees only the individual expenses they are part of.

Anyone signed in to SplitFree can find you by name or username, and see your name, username and picture in the search results, so they can send you a friend request. They see nothing else until you share an expense with them. Scanning your QR code only sends you a request; it gives no access by itself.

When you file a report about another user, the report, with both usernames and the reason you give, is emailed to the developer so it can be looked at. The person you report is not told who reported them.

Notification text can include an amount and a name, for example a payment reminder. If your phone shows notification content on the lock screen, anyone holding the phone can see it. You can change this in Android under Settings, Notifications, Notifications on lock screen.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept for as long as your account exists, and removed when you delete it, as described in the next section. Crash reports are deleted by Sentry after 90 days. The log of reminders sent is cleared out regularly. Routine encrypted backups at Supabase can hold a copy for a few days before they are overwritten.

Deleting your account

In the app, go to Account, then Settings, then Delete account. Any open balance has to be settled first, so nobody is left with a debt pointing at a deleted person. Deletion happens straight away and cannot be undone; there is no waiting period in which the account can be restored.

What is deleted: your account and sign-in, your profile (name, username, email address, UPI ID and picture), every friendship together with the expenses and settlements inside it, your shop payments, activity history, feedback, comments, reports you filed, invites, celebration GIFs, notification tokens, and the payment screenshots and photo you uploaded.

What is kept, and why: expenses and settlements in a group stay, so that every other member's balance stays correct. They keep showing the display name you used, but they are no longer linked to any account and hold no email address, UPI ID or picture. A group you own passes to its longest-standing member, or is deleted if nobody else is in it. Anonymised group entries stay for as long as the group's other members keep their records.

If you cannot get into the app, email justutsav.05@gmail.com from the address you signed up with, and your account will be deleted for you within 30 days.

Your rights

You can see and correct your details in the app at any time, and delete your account as above. Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you can also ask for a summary of the personal data SplitFree holds about you and who it has been shared with, ask for it to be corrected or erased, and raise a complaint. Write to the address below; requests are answered within 30 days. If you are not satisfied with the answer, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Security

All traffic is encrypted. Photos and screenshots are stored privately and shown through links that expire after a few minutes. Every permission check happens on the server, so data you are not part of is never sent to your phone in the first place. There are no passwords to leak: sign-in is a one-time code or your Google account.

Children

SplitFree is not meant for children under 13, and does not knowingly collect their data. If you are under 18, use SplitFree only with a parent's or guardian's permission. If you believe a child has signed up, write to the address below and the account will be removed.

This website

splitfree.justutsav.com sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other companies: its fonts and code are served from the site itself. The hosting provider, Vercel, keeps standard server logs, such as internet addresses and browser types, for security and to keep the site running.
